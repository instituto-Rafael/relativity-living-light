## Assistant response — normalized preservation

The key distinction established was that a “brick” is not a single fact but a structured subgraph:

\[
B_i=(V_i,E_i,P_i,C_i,U_i)
\]

with facts/claims, internal relations, provenance, context/conditions and uncertainty.

A relation such as:

\[
B_2\leftrightarrow B_7
\]

must not be read as identity of the whole bricks. It can instead be a typed relation between subparts:

\[
B_{2.3}\leftrightarrow B_{7.5}
\]

This yields three distinct novelty classes:

1. **Representational conversion**
   \[
   3+3+3+3\equiv4\times3
   \]
   with semantic preservation.

2. **Structural integration**
   \[
   A\leftrightarrow B\leftrightarrow C
   \]
   where the novelty is in the organization of already-known facts.

3. **New relation**
   \[
   R^\*(B_i,B_j)
   \]
   which becomes a hypothesis if the relation itself has not yet been demonstrated.

The session introduced:

\[
K=(V,E),\qquad K'=(V,E\cup\{R^\*\})
\]

so that a validated new edge can increase knowledge even when no new node is added:

\[
K'\neq K
\]

This was named provisionally:

\[
\Omega_{\mathrm{lift}}:(V,E)\rightarrow(V,E\cup R^\*)
\]

or **Relational Knowledge Lift**.

The operational route proposed was:

\[
READ\rightarrow ATOMIZE\rightarrow RELATE\rightarrow TRANSFORM\rightarrow COMPARE\rightarrow FALSIFY\rightarrow INTEGRATE
\]

with every relation represented as:

\[
R_{ij}=\langle type,mechanism,source,confidence,falsifier\rangle
\]

and unresolved relations mapped to \`TOKEN_VAZIO\`.
