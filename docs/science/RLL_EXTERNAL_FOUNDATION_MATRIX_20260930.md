### Chia as a transversal evidence-method example

This material is deliberately outside the RLL cosmology evidence chain. It is useful only to illustrate how a claim becomes strong by multiple constrained studies rather than by one narrative.

The animal-feed review reports trials where chia supplementation can increase omega-3 content in animal products. A 30% inclusion trial exists for laying hens, but high doses are not a universal optimum; performance effects vary by strain/species/dose.

Composition reviews support roughly:

```text
protein             ~ 15–25% mass
dietary fibre       ~ 30–34% mass
insoluble fraction  ~ 85–93% of the fibre fraction
magnesium            ~ 335–449 mg / 100 g
oil                  ~ 30–40%
major FA             ALA (omega-3), LA (omega-6), oleic acid (omega-9)
```

Thus “73% fibre” is not supported as total dietary fibre by the reviewed sources. It may reflect confusion between total fibre and the high insoluble fraction of that fibre.

For linseed, the EFSA evidence confirms cyanogenic glycosides capable of releasing HCN. However, the reproductive rat study located in this review did **not** report impaired fetal development at the tested flaxseed/flaxseed-meal levels. The specific fetal-malformation statement therefore remains unverified rather than being used as a premise.
